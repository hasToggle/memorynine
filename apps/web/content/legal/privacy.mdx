## The short version

memorynine records what your team says after a client call, turns it into a
memory the team can question, and shows you where every answer came from.

- **Your workspace content is yours.** Recordings, transcripts, forwarded mail
  and the facts drawn out of them belong to your company. We hold them on your
  instructions and for no other purpose.
- **Where it lives.** Recordings, transcripts and the knowledge base sit in EU
  regions. Speech-to-text runs on an EU endpoint.
- **Where it does not always live.** Language-model inference is routed through
  a gateway that fails over between providers, so an individual request can be
  processed outside the EU. Those providers retain nothing and train on nothing.
  [Details below](#where-data-is-processed).
- **What never reaches us.** Bank details, card numbers, passport and licence
  numbers, health conditions, medication and injuries are removed during
  transcription, before anything is stored.
- **We do not train models on your content.** Neither do the providers we call.
- **Erasing a person is real.** One action removes their facts, redacts them
  out of the transcripts and the review history, and marks their recordings for
  deletion. It cannot be undone.

The rest of this page is the detail behind those points.

## Two different roles

Which rules apply depends on whose data it is. We are in one of two positions,
and the difference matters for your rights and for ours.

| What                                                                              | Our role      | Who decides what happens to it |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Visitors to this website                                                          | **Controller** | Us                             |
| People who ask for early access or a walkthrough                                  | **Controller** | Us                             |
| Account holders — your name, email, which workspace you belong to                 | **Controller** | Us                             |
| Billing records                                                                   | **Controller** | Us                             |
| Everything inside a workspace — recordings, transcripts, forwarded mail, the people and companies they describe, the facts drawn out of them | **Processor** | Your company                   |

That last row is most of the product. Your company decides what goes in, what

else. If you are one of our customers' clients and want to know what is held
about you, the company you deal with is the right place to ask — and we help
them answer.

## What we process as controller

### When you visit this site

We process the request data your browser sends: IP address, user agent,
referring page, and the page you asked for. This is needed to serve the site
and to keep it standing up against abuse. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our
legitimate interest in operating and defending a website.

Product analytics run only with your consent, and the site works without them.
See [Cookies and analytics](#cookies-and-analytics).

### When you ask for early access

The form takes your email address. Before we store it, the address is checked
for format, against a list of disposable-mail domains, and against a
deliverability service — so the confirmation mail actually arrives and the list
is not used to bounce traffic at somebody else. We then send you a link, and
nothing else happens until you click it.

We keep your address, the confirmation status, and the timestamps. Legal basis:
Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (your consent, given by confirming), and Art. 6(1)(f) for the
validity check. You can withdraw at any time by replying to any message from us
or writing to the privacy contact above; we delete the address.

### When you hold an account

better-auth stores your name, email address, a hashed password, your email
verification state, your sessions, which organisations you belong to and your
role in them, and any invitations you sent or received. Legal basis:
Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR — performing the contract you signed up for.

### Operating the service

We record one row per model call — which operation, how many tokens, what it
cost, and an identifier linking it to the source that triggered it. These rows
carry no content from your workspace. They exist for billing and for spotting a
worker that has started burning money. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) and (f).

## What we process as processor

Everything below is workspace content. Your company is the controller; we act
on its instructions.

### Sources

- **Voice memos.** The audio file, its duration and content type, who recorded
  it, and when. Audio goes into private storage: it is not publicly reachable,
  and every read requires a signed link that expires.
- **Notes.** Whatever somebody typed or pasted.
- **Forwarded email.** The body, the original sender, who forwarded it, the
  subject, the message id and the date it was sent. Only addresses on your
  workspace's allowlist can post mail into it.
- **Transcripts.** The text produced from a voice memo, plus the detected
  language.

### What is removed before storage

Speech-to-text is instructed to redact these categories at the provider, so
they never reach our database at all:

account numbers, banking information, blood type, credit card number, expiry
and CVV, driver's licence numbers, drugs and medication, healthcare numbers,
injuries, medical conditions, medical procedures, passport numbers, and social
security numbers.

Names, companies and money amounts deliberately remain. They are the signal the
product exists to keep. If your team needs a category kept out that is not on
this list, tell us — the list is configuration, not architecture.

### Entities and facts

From a source, the extraction step drafts:

- **People** — name, email addresses, role, which organisation they belong to.
- **Organisations** — name, domains, industry, whether they are a lead, active
  or former.
- **Engagements** — title, type, status, start and end dates.
- **Facts** — a sentence about one of the above, its category, a confidence
  score, when it became true and when it stopped, and the source it came from.

Nothing in that list becomes part of the knowledge base until a person in your
workspace confirms it. Until then it sits as a draft in the review queue.

### The review trail

We keep who confirmed, edited or discarded each draft, and when. Without it
there is no way to answer "who decided this was true", which is the question
that makes the rest of the record worth anything.

## Where data is processed

| Component                                | Where                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Application, functions, static hosting   | EU regions                                                        |
| Database (accounts and knowledge base)   | EU region                                                         |
| Audio and attachment storage             | EU region, private, signed access only                            |
| Speech-to-text                           | EU endpoint                                                       |
| Language-model inference                 | Routed via a gateway; the serving region is not guaranteed        |
| Reranking of search results              | Processed outside the EU                                          |

The last two rows are the honest caveat, and we would rather you read it here
than discover it in a procurement review.

**Language-model inference.** Extraction, consolidation, contradiction checks
and answering your questions all call a language model through Vercel AI
Gateway. The gateway routes to whichever provider is healthy, which means an
individual request can be served outside the EU. Every provider we route to
operates under zero data retention: the request is processed and nothing is
kept, and nothing is used for training. What is sent is the text needed for
that one call — a transcript to be extracted from, or the facts relevant to a
question — never your whole knowledge base.

**Reranking.** When a question is asked, candidate facts are re-scored by a
cross-encoder that runs outside the EU. What is sent is the question and the
candidate fact texts. Nothing is retained.

Transfers outside the EU rest on the European Commission's standard
contractual clauses together with the transfer mechanisms our providers
maintain, and on Art. 46 GDPR. If your organisation cannot accept
model inference leaving the EU, say so before you start — it is a
configuration question, not a fixed property of the product, and we would
rather have that conversation up front.

## Erasure

When somebody exercises their right under Art. 17 GDPR, your workspace can
erase them in one action. That does the following, and we mention it here
because "we will delete your data" usually means less than people assume:

- Every fact anchored to that person is deleted, including facts that were
  consolidated out of them, which an anchor-scoped delete would otherwise miss.
- Facts about other people that still mention them by name are redacted.
- Their name and addresses are removed from the source transcripts and from the
  review history, so the record that a meeting happened survives without them
  in it.
- Recordings and attachments that no longer back any surviving fact are marked
  for deletion, and the stored files go with them.
- Derived caches built from any of the above are dropped and rebuilt.

It is irreversible. There is no recycle bin, because a recycle bin would mean
we kept a copy.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask for access to your data (Art. 15), correction
(Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), a portable copy
(Art. 20), and you can object to processing based on legitimate interests
(Art. 21). Where processing rests on consent, you can withdraw it at any time,
without affecting what was lawful before you did.

Write to the privacy contact above. We answer within one month.

**One routing note.** For anything inside a customer's workspace, that customer
is the controller, not us. If you are a client of a company that uses
memorynine, ask them — they can see the record, they can correct it, and they
can erase you. If you approach us instead, we will pass the request on and tell
you we have.

## Automated decision-making

There is none within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR. The assistant drafts and
proposes; a person confirms. Nothing it produces has a legal or similarly
significant effect on anyone by itself, and nothing enters the knowledge base
without a human saying so.

The assistant can be wrong, and it is built to say when it does not know rather
than fill the gap. Answers carry the source they rest on so a reader can check
them. That is a design decision, not a legal guarantee: what your team repeats
to a client is your team's responsibility.

## Cookies and analytics

Strictly necessary cookies — your session, your consent choice — are set
without asking, because the site does not work without them. Everything else
waits for your consent, and you can withdraw it at any time. Legal basis for
non-essential cookies and similar technologies: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR together with
§ 25(1) TDDDG.

## Security

Access to a workspace is scoped on every single read, from the signed-in
session rather than from anything a user or a model can influence — so there is
no question that reaches another workspace's data. Recordings sit in private
storage reachable only through links that expire. Passwords are hashed.
Transport is encrypted. Material arriving from outside — a forwarded thread, a
transcript — is treated as text somebody wrote, never as an instruction to the
assistant.
